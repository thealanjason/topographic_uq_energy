Introduction

Figure 1: Original vs. Noisy DEM

The Impact of DEM Uncertainty

“Overlooking DEM uncertainty may lead to a bias in risk management decisions” [1]

The Physical Link

  • Gravity-Driven: Models use 3D maps to calculate how water, mud, and rocks pull downhill.
  • Direct Impact: Any variation in the map’s shape can significantly alter the resulting mathematical prediction [1].

The Solution

  • Probabilistic Approach: We use an “ensemble of equiprobable realizations” [1].
  • Scale: 500 realizations are generated to quantify the range of possible outcomes [1].

Motivation

The Computational Burden of Uncertainty

  • Stochastic Scale: Generating an ensemble of 500 realizations requires massive computational throughput.
  • The Energy Footprint: Moving from standard desktop research to High-Performance Computing (HPC) drastically increases power consumption.
  • Resource Intensity: Running these simulations demands significant electricity, driving the need for benchmarking and optimization.

Is Execution Time a Reliable Proxy?

  • Hardware Heterogeneity: CPUs and GPUs have fundamentally different power profiles.
  • Non-Linearity: Doubling the execution time does not necessarily double the energy consumption.
  • Complex Scaling: Power draw varies based on clock speeds, memory bandwidth, and thermal throttling.

The Attribution Challenge

  • Concurrent Workloads: Modern hardware (both local and HPC) often runs multiple tasks at the same time.
  • Sensor Limitations: Hardware sensors report the total power of the card, not the energy used by a specific Process ID (PID).
  • Measurement Noise: Background system tasks “pollute” the data, making it hard to see the true cost of just our simulation.

Objective: Develop a methodology to attribute energy consumption strictly to our specific simulation process, independent of other system activity.

Research Questions

  • How can the energy consumption of heterogeneous scientific computing workloads be accurately measured and modeled under input and execution uncertainty?
    • To what extent can execution time serve as a proxy for energy consumption in heterogeneous CPU–GPU computing systems?
    • How does uncertainty in simulation inputs propagate to variability in computational energy consumption?
    • What methodologies enable accurate process-level energy attribution in shared heterogeneous computing infrastructures?

Methodology

  • Solver: Synxflow
  • Soft Sensor: Alumet
